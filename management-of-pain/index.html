The short answer

Management of pain is a long-term, drug-free plan for pain that has lasted three months or more. It combines hands-on chiropractic care, soft-tissue work, guided movement and practical daily habits, with clear goals you set and we track together.

Living with ongoing pain wears on more than your body. It changes how you sleep, how patient you feel, and how often you say no to things you used to enjoy. This page explains how we approach pain that has stuck around, what a plan looks like here, and what you can realistically expect.

What makes long-term pain different?

Short-term pain usually follows a clear event, like a lift that went wrong, and it tends to fade as the tissue heals. Pain that lasts for months often works differently. The original strain may have settled, but the body has learned to guard, stiffen and brace around the area, and the nervous system can stay on high alert. That is why rest alone rarely solves it, and why the same flare keeps coming back.

That does not mean the pain is in your head. It means the plan has to address movement, muscle tension, daily load and confidence together, rather than one sore spot at a time.

The kinds of long-term pain we most often help people manage:

  • Low back pain that flares with sitting, lifting or long days on your feet.
  • Neck and upper back tension that rides up into headaches.
  • Pain that started after a car accident or a work injury and never fully settled.
  • Hip, shoulder and knee pain tied to how you move and load the joint.
  • Stiffness and soreness that make mornings or long drives harder than they should be.

How do we help you manage pain without drugs?

Care at {{clinic_name}} does not involve narcotic pain medicine or injections. Instead, the plan works on the mechanics and habits behind the pain, so you depend less on anything, including us, over time. Depending on what your exam shows, that plan can include:

  • Chiropractic adjustments matched in force to your body, to restore motion to stiff, guarded joints.
  • Massage therapy and soft-tissue work to ease the muscle tension that builds around a painful area.
  • Corrective therapy, with guided stretching and exercise that rebuild strength and range of motion.
  • Kinesiology taping to support a sore area during daily activity.
  • Simple home movement you can do in a few minutes a day.
  • Practical changes to how you sit, sleep, lift and pace your day.

If your history or exam points to something that needs a medical work-up first, we tell you plainly and help you find the right provider. Part of managing pain well is knowing when it is not a chiropractic problem.

What does a pain management plan look like here?

  1. Your story first We start with how the pain began, what makes it better or worse, what you have already tried, and what it is stopping you from doing.
  2. A careful exam We check how you move, where you are guarded, and what your joints and muscles are doing, so care is based on findings rather than guesses.
  3. Goals you choose Instead of chasing a pain number, we agree on real goals, like sleeping through the night, finishing a shift, or getting back to the gym or the trail.
  4. A focused course of care Visits are usually closer together at first, while we settle the flare and build movement, then spaced out as you need us less.
  5. A plan for flares You leave with a short list of what to do when pain spikes, so a bad day does not undo weeks of progress.

What can you realistically expect?

Honest expectations matter with long-term pain. Many people notice that flares become less intense or less frequent, that they move with more confidence, and that they can do more before pain shows up. Some people reach a point where pain rarely interrupts their week. Others learn to keep it at a level that no longer runs their life. We cannot promise a specific outcome, and we will tell you if progress stalls and a different approach makes more sense.

What does care cost, and do you take insurance?

No referral is needed to book. We take most health insurance plans and offer free insurance verification, so you know where you stand before your first visit. If you are paying out of pocket, we go over the costs with you up front.

  • No referral needed to book a first visit.

Questions

Questions about managing long-term pain

Do you prescribe pain medication?

No. Care at our office does not involve narcotic pain medicine or injections. If you are already taking medication from your doctor, keep following their advice; we work alongside it and never ask you to stop a prescription.

My pain has lasted for years. Is it too late to help?

Not necessarily. Many people with long-standing pain still respond well to care that restores movement and reduces guarding. We will give you an honest view after your exam, including if we think another provider is a better fit.

How long will I need care?

Most plans start with a focused course of visits over several weeks, then taper. How long depends on how long the pain has been building and how it responds. Ongoing check-ins are always your choice, never a package you are locked into.

Will treatment hurt?

Care is matched to your body and how sensitive the area is that day. Low-force options are available, and you can ask us to change or stop anything at any time.

What should I bring to my first visit?

A list of medications, any recent imaging or reports, and notes on when your pain is worse or better. The more detail you bring, the clearer your plan will be.
