The short answer

Corrective therapy is hands-on and movement-based care that supports your chiropractic adjustments. It uses manual therapy, myofascial work, assisted stretching and guided exercise to ease tension, restore mobility and build the strength that keeps pain from returning.

An adjustment can free up a stiff joint, but the muscles and habits around that joint decide whether the change sticks. Corrective therapy works on those muscles and habits. Here is what it involves, who it helps, what a session is like, and how it fits with the rest of your care.

What is corrective therapy?

Corrective therapy is a form of medically directed rehab. Your chiropractor sets the goals, and our corrective therapy team helps you reach them through hands-on work and coached movement. The aim is to restore function, improve mobility, ease pain, and reduce the chance of the same problem coming back.

Depending on your needs, a session can include:

  • Manual therapy, using hands-on techniques to ease tight, guarded muscles.
  • Myofascial work to release tension in the connective tissue around muscles.
  • Assisted stretching, where a team member guides you through stretches you could not do as well alone.
  • Guided exercise to rebuild strength, balance and control, progressed as you improve.
  • Kinesiology taping to support an area between visits.
  • Simple home exercises so progress continues between sessions.

Who is corrective therapy for?

Corrective therapy can help people whose pain or stiffness keeps returning, or who need more than an adjustment to get back to full activity. We often recommend it for:

  • Low back and neck pain that flares again soon after an adjustment.
  • Recovery after a car accident, sports injury or work injury.
  • Tight hips, shoulders and hamstrings that limit how you move.
  • Posture-related strain from desk work, driving or carrying gear.
  • Weakness or poor control around a joint that keeps getting hurt.

If your exam shows a problem that needs a medical work-up or a different kind of rehab, we tell you and help you find the right provider.

How does corrective therapy work with your adjustments?

Think of the adjustment as opening a door and corrective therapy as teaching your body to walk through it. After an adjustment restores motion to a joint, the muscle work and stretching ease the tension that was pulling it back out of place. Guided exercise then builds the strength and control to hold that improvement during real life, like lifting a child, finishing a shift, or getting through a long drive.

What happens in a corrective therapy session?

  1. A quick check-in We ask how you have felt since your last visit and what your chiropractor wants to focus on.
  2. Hands-on work Manual therapy and myofascial work to loosen the areas that are tight and guarded.
  3. Assisted stretching Guided stretches that improve range of motion safely, at a pace that suits you.
  4. Guided exercise Movements chosen for your goals, from gentle activation early on to more challenging strength work later.
  5. Your home plan A few simple exercises to do between visits, updated as you progress.

Questions

Corrective therapy questions we hear most

Is corrective therapy the same as physical therapy?

It is similar in approach, using hands-on work and guided exercise, but at our office it is directed by your chiropractor and coordinated with your adjustments. If you need a separate physical therapy referral, we can help with that.

Do I need a chiropractic exam first?

Yes. Your chiropractor examines you first and sets the goals, so your therapy sessions are aimed at what is actually driving your pain.

Will the stretching hurt?

You may feel a stretch or mild soreness, but it should not be painful. We work within your comfort and adjust the pressure and range as you go. Tell us anytime something does not feel right.

What should I wear?

Comfortable clothes you can move and stretch in, like athletic wear, and supportive shoes.

How long is a session?

Session length depends on your plan. We will tell you what to expect when we schedule your first corrective therapy visit.
